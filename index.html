Kashinoga Design System

Human interface guidelines for the web, and the CSS that implements them. One entry point, styles.css, in six cascade layers: reset, tokens, base, layout, components, utilities.

Status: scaffold. The foundations below are neutral placeholders with the final structure in place. Nothing has been migrated from Pixelite yet.

Foundations

Colour

One accent plus three answering inks. The accent means you are here — a state, never an outcome. The three answers are named as stones, and that constraint is what stops the palette growing a fifth ambiguous shade. Every swatch is read live from the stylesheet, so this table cannot drift from the source.

Type

Four faces, four jobs, no overlap. A reader should be able to tell what kind of thing they are looking at before reading it. Every stack defaults to a face the platform already provides — no webfont, no network request, no flash of unstyled text. Body and display agree for now because no platform sets its headings in a serif; they stay separate tokens so one can move without the other.

Space

Space is the primary means of expression here. Grouping, delineation and separation — the work a border would normally do — are done with space, and a border is what you reach for only when space cannot reach. A rule says a boundary once, in one colour, and then has to be maintained in every theme it survives into; a gap says it in every theme, at every contrast setting, and in print, for free.

Three steps, in order — take the first that works and stop. Space groups, separates and ranks; it is the answer for nearly everything. Colour takes over when distance cannot carry it: no gap makes a word look pressable, and sticky chrome has no fixed distance to the content it must be told apart from, but a fill of its own separates it at any scroll offset. A border only when the boundary is the content, and no fill can supply an edge because the fill is the subject. The colour swatches above looked like that case — white chips on a white sheet — until the card behind them was tinted instead. There is no border anywhere on this page.

[s], the floor

At least one [s] sits between any two elements. Nothing that separates two things may go below it. It is a floor and not a default because the failure is one-sided: too much air is a judgement you can disagree with, but too little is a rendering fault — the eye reads the pair as one object and the structure of the page disappears. With no borders left to recover it, there is nothing to fall back on.

Everything else counts in whole units. --space is one unit; --space-x2 is two. The x matters: --space-2 is already the 2px rung, so a count could otherwise be read as a measurement. --gap-tight is the one value below the floor, and it is not a separation — a label and its field are one control, not two elements.

Grouping

A rung is a size; these are relationships, and they are what actually replaces the border. Proximity only groups if the gaps are clearly unequal — two groups 10px apart with 8px inside them are not two groups, they are one group with a wobble. So each tier must be at least twice the one below it, and that ratio is asserted.

Document rhythm

The prose axis. The grouping tiers rank parts of an interface; this sets the beat of a document you read from top to bottom. Everything gets one unit. A new h1 gets two. A heading under a heading stays close, because the pair is one title block. Put .prose on a container and its blocks space themselves. 

H1
[s]
P
[s]
P
[s]
[s]      <- two units: a heading after content starts something new
H2
[s]
H3       <- one unit: a heading under a heading is one title block
[s]
P
[s]

.prose has no tokens of its own — it reads --space and --space-x2 directly, because two names for one value is drift waiting to happen. The doubled gap is the only signal that a new section starts, so the multiple is exact: a value that merely looks bigger would make the section break a matter of opinion.

The scale

2px base, and the name is the pixel count — --space-12 is 12px at a 16px root. A rung whose name lies is worse than no name, so the identity is asserted in test/tokens.test.js. Strides widen as the scale climbs, because a 2px difference is decisive at 8px and invisible at 64px; they may never narrow.

Structure

One class, on the article. Everything inside is plain semantic markup — an author writes a document, not a set of class names, and the structure stays legible in the source with no stylesheet to decode it. 

<article class="document">
  <header>    the document banner — title, authors, abstract
  <section>   a division of the document
    <p> <figure><figcaption> <aside>
  <footer>    colophon, references, revision date
</article>

The article's own <header> is the banner; they are not two elements. A banner outside the article is not part of the document — a reader who copies the article loses the title, and a screen reader announcing the article starts at the first paragraph.

<section> holds the paragraphs and figures. It cannot itself be a paragraph: a <p> may not contain a <figure>, and the parser does not complain — it closes the paragraph, lifts the figure out, and leaves a stray empty <p> behind. Your CSS then styles a tree you did not write.

A note belongs beside the line that raises it. An <aside> is an ordinary block in the flow until the container is wide enough to hold a margin column without stealing from the measure. That is a container query, not a media query — a document in a narrow pane on a wide screen has a narrow measure and wants the inline form.

Components

Nothing here yet, deliberately. The components layer exists and its place in the cascade is settled, so the first component can land without reordering anything. Conventions for what goes in it are documented at the top of src/components.css.

Principles

  1. Say only what cannot be guessed. The audience is technical. Do not caption standard affordances; do document what the platform will not allow.
  2. A control that cannot act is absent, not disabled. Greying out asks the reader to work out why; removing states it.
  3. A value copied is a value that will drift. Publish it as a custom property and read it.
  4. A gap is not a space. Spacing a unit or a legend with margin or gap produces text that reads as one word to a screen reader and on copy-paste. Use real characters.
  5. Movement is a feature; a printed manual does not bounce. Under reduced-motion, keep the state change and drop the sweep — somebody who asked for less movement did not ask for less information.
