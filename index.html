Kashinoga Design System

Human interface guidelines for the web, and the CSS that implements them. One entry point, styles.css, in six cascade layers: reset, tokens, base, layout, components, utilities.

Scaffold

The foundations below are neutral placeholders with the final structure in place. Nothing from Pixelite is here yet.

Foundations

Space

Space is the primary means of expression in this system. Space does the work that a border usually does. It groups items, it divides them, and it ranks them. Use a border only when space cannot do the work.

A rule states a boundary one time, in one colour. You must then keep that colour correct in each theme that the rule stays in. A gap states the same boundary in each theme, at each contrast setting, and in print. A gap costs nothing.

There are three steps, in this order. Take the first step that works, then stop.

Space groups, divides and ranks. It is the answer for almost all cases.

Colour takes over when distance cannot do the work. No gap can make a word look pressable. Sticky chrome has no fixed distance from the content, but a fill of its own divides it at each scroll position.

A border is correct only when the boundary is the content, and no fill can supply an edge. This occurs when the fill is the subject. The colour swatches below looked like this case: white chips on a white sheet. A tint on the card behind them removed the need for the border.

This page draws lines in three places, and each one is the same case. The rule between the wordmark and the descriptor in the bar above is a separator. The break above each section title is a separator. Both have one function only: to be a boundary. There is nothing else for them to be made of.

The third place is the rules in the tables below. A table wide enough that the eye loses its row between the first column and the last is a tracking problem, not a grouping problem, and space cannot answer it. That case is named in src/base.css, which rules no table itself and leaves the decision to the component.

The Quantum

At least one [s] goes between any two elements. Nothing that divides two things can go below this value. It is a floor, not a default, because the failure is one-sided. Too much air is a judgement that you can disagree with. Too little air is a fault: the eye reads the two items as one object, and the structure of the page disappears. No borders remain to correct it, so there is nothing to fall back on.

All other values count in whole units. --space is one unit. --space-x2 is two units. The x is necessary, because --space-2 is already the 2px rung. Without the x, you could read a count as a measurement.

--gap-tight is the one value below the floor, and it is not a division. A label and its field are one control, not two elements.

Grouping

A rung is a size. These tiers are relationships, and they are what replaces the border. Proximity groups items only if the gaps are clearly unequal. Two groups 10px apart with 8px inside them are not two groups. They are one group with a fault in it. Each tier must therefore be at least twice the tier below it. A test asserts this ratio.

Document rhythm

This is the prose axis. The grouping tiers rank the parts of an interface. This rhythm sets the beat of a document that you read from top to bottom. Each block goes two units below the block before it. Headings are not a special case. Put .prose on a container, and the blocks inside it space themselves.

There is one number, and no ratio to learn. An earlier model ranked the gaps. It put one unit below a heading and four units above it, so that a heading stayed nearer to the content it named than to the content before it. That model worked. It was replaced on purpose, to get a flat column.

The change has a cost. A heading now sits at an equal distance from the block above and the block below. Only its size and its weight show what it is attached to. Space no longer helps.

A group is not a block. This rhythm applies between blocks. The items inside one group stay at --gap-within. List items, the faces above and the swatches below are all such items. Proximity groups items only while the gap inside a group stays clearly smaller than the gap around it.

.prose has no tokens of its own. It reads --space-x2 directly, because two names for one value will cause drift.

The scale

The base is 2px, and the name of each rung is its pixel count. --space-12 is 12px at a 16px root. A rung with a name that lies is worse than a rung with no name, so test/tokens.test.js asserts the identity. The steps get larger as the scale climbs, because a 2px difference is clear at 8px and invisible at 64px. The steps must never get smaller.

Type

There are four faces and four jobs. No face takes a second job. A reader must be able to see what kind of item they are looking at before they read it.

Each stack defaults to a face that the platform supplies. There is no webfont, no network request, and no flash of unstyled text. Body and display use the same stack at present, because no platform sets its headings in a serif. They stay separate tokens, so that one can change without the other.

Colour

There is one accent and three answering inks. The accent means you are here. It states a condition, never a result. The three answers have the names of stones. That constraint stops the palette from growing a fifth unclear shade. The page reads each swatch from the live stylesheet, so this table cannot drift from the source.

Structure

There is one class, and it goes on the wrapper. All the markup inside it is plain and semantic. An author writes a document, not a set of class names, and the structure stays clear in the source with no stylesheet to decode it.

The sample below is real markup, not a sketch. A test parses it and checks that the browser returns the tree it was given. 

<div class="document">
  <nav aria-label="Contents">
    <ol>
      <li><a href="#division">A division</a></li>
    </ol>
  </nav>

  <article>
    <!-- The banner: title, authors, abstract. -->
    <header>
      <h1>Title</h1>
      <p>Authors</p>
    </header>

    <section id="division">
      <h2>A division</h2>
      <p>A paragraph.</p>

      <figure>
        <img src="chart.svg" alt="" />
        <figcaption>Figure 1</figcaption>
      </figure>

      <aside>A note or a citation.</aside>
    </section>

    <footer>
      <p>Colophon, references, revision date.</p>
    </footer>
  </article>
</div>

The article's own <header> is the banner. They are not two elements. A banner outside the article is not part of the document. A reader who copies the article then loses the title, and a screen reader that announces the article starts at the first paragraph.

<section> holds the paragraphs and the figures. It cannot itself be a paragraph, because a <p> cannot contain a <figure>. The parser does not report this. It closes the paragraph, moves the figure out, and leaves an empty <p> behind. Your CSS then styles a tree that you did not write.

A note belongs beside the line that causes it. An <aside> stays a usual block in the flow until the container is wide enough for a margin column. The margin column must not take width from the measure. Use a container query for this, not a media query. A document in a narrow pane on a wide screen has a narrow measure, and it needs the inline form.

Components

Nothing here yet, deliberately. The components layer exists and its place in the cascade is settled, so the first component can land without reordering anything. Conventions for what goes in it are documented at the top of src/components.css.

Principles

  1. Say only what cannot be guessed.
    1. The audience is technical. Do not caption standard affordances; do document what the platform will not allow.
  2. A control that cannot act is absent, not disabled.
    1. Greying out asks the reader to work out why; removing states it.
  3. A value copied is a value that will drift.
    1. Publish it as a custom property and read it.
  4. A gap is not a space.
    1. Spacing a unit or a legend with margin or gap produces text that reads as one word to a screen reader and on copy-paste. Use real characters.
  5. Movement is a feature; a printed manual does not bounce.
    1. Under reduced motion, keep the state change and remove the sweep. A person who asked for less movement did not ask for less information.
